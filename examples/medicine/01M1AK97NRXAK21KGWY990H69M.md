# Serum anion gap

What does it estimate, and how is it calculated?

<!-- hint -->
Only routinely measured ions appear in the formula.

<!-- back -->
It estimates the anions that a standard electrolyte panel does not measure, since plasma as a whole must be electrically neutral.

$$AG = [Na^+] - ([Cl^-] + [HCO_3^-])$$

Potassium is normally left out: its concentration is small and tightly regulated, so including it changes the number very little. Reference intervals differ between laboratories and depend on the method used for sodium and chloride.

A raised gap means an unmeasured anion has accumulated — lactate and ketoacids are the usual examples. A metabolic acidosis with a normal gap points instead to loss of bicarbonate or retention of chloride.

<!-- note -->
Albumin is the largest single contributor to the normal gap, so a low albumin lowers the expected value.
