# The ventricular action potential

A working ventricular myocyte rests near -90 mV, and its action potential is conventionally divided into five phases numbered 0 to 4. Each phase is the net result of whichever ion currents are open at that moment.

The plateau is what distinguishes it: the whole action potential lasts on the order of two to three hundred milliseconds, far longer than one in nerve or skeletal muscle, and the ventricle cannot be re-excited for most of that time.

Pacemaker cells of the sinoatrial node are described with the same numbering but have no stable phase 4.
