# が in the opening line of a folk tale

むかしむかし、あるところに、おじいさんとおばあさんが住んでいました。

Why が and not は on おじいさんとおばあさん?

<!-- back -->
The old couple are being brought into the story for the first time, and が marks a subject that is new to the discourse. Once they are established, the following sentences take them up again with は.

<!-- note -->
Makino and Tsutsui, A Dictionary of Basic Japanese Grammar, entries は and が.
